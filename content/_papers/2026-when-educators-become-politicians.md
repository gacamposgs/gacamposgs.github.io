Political incentives to raise school quality are often weak in decentralized education systems. In this context, officeholders with professional knowledge of education and strong attachment to the sector may shape public-service delivery. This paper examines the effects of electing mayors with education-sector backgrounds (hereafter, educators) on student learning and teacher wages in Brazilian municipal elections from 2004 to 2016. I use a politician-characteristic regression discontinuity design that compares municipalities where educator candidates barely win with those where they barely lose. Test-score improvements under educator mayors' terms are modest and increasing, rising from 0.005 to 0.068 standard deviations, but are imprecisely estimated. Notably, the largest learning gains occur five years after the election, after the next municipal election, when test scores rise by 0.16 standard deviations. I find no evidence that educator mayors generate broad wage gains for teachers. Consistent with this finding, there is also no detectable effect on total municipal spending on education. Instead, the evidence better aligns with changes in school personnel, particularly greater stability and experience of school principals. Overall, the findings suggest that politicians' professional backgrounds in education can influence local public service delivery through changes in school leadership.
